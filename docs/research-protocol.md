# Research protocol (v0.1)

Committed before `docs/research-report.md` was written, and before the registry in
`reports/v0.1-wormhole-registry.json` was interpreted. This protocol has no dataset to fit
against — the "result" is the exact, deterministic evaluation of stated closed-form equations,
so what is preregistered here is the equations themselves, their literature source, and the
independent checks that must pass, not a data-fitting procedure.

## Research question

Does a static, spherically symmetric, horizon-free ("traversable") wormhole exist as a
consistent solution of Einstein's field equations — and if so, precisely what does the field
equations force the matter threading its throat to be?

## The metric under study

Following Morris & Thorne, *American Journal of Physics* **56**, 395 (1988) ("Wormholes in
spacetime and their use for interstellar travel"), the metric studied throughout this repository
is, in geometrized units (G = c = 1) and Schwarzschild-like coordinates (t, r, θ, φ):

```
ds^2 = -e^{2 Phi(r)} c^2 dt^2 + dr^2 / (1 - b(r)/r) + r^2 (dtheta^2 + sin^2(theta) dphi^2)
```

`Phi(r)` is the *redshift function* and `b(r)` is the *shape function*. A wormhole solution
requires, at the throat radius r0:

- `b(r0) = r0`
- the **flare-out condition**: `b'(r0) < 1` (the embedded spatial slice must curve outward,
  not pinch shut)
- `Phi(r)` finite everywhere (no coordinate or curvature singularity — this is precisely what
  makes the solution *traversable* rather than a one-way black-hole-like horizon)

## Functions tested (disclosed before any output was generated)

**Shape function family** (`src/wormhole_lab/metrics.py::ShapeFunction`):

```
b(r) = r0^(n+1) / r^n
```

tested at `n in {0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}` and `r0 in {1.0, 5.0}`. `n = 1` reproduces the original
Morris & Thorne worked example, `b(r) = r0^2/r`. `n = 0` is the constant "thin-shell" case,
`b(r) = r0`. For this family, `b'(r0) = -n`, so flare-out (`b'(r0) < 1`) holds for every
`n > -1`, in particular for every value tested here.

**Redshift function presets** (`src/wormhole_lab/metrics.py::RedshiftFunction`):

- `"zero"`: `Phi(r) = 0` everywhere — the "zero-tidal-force" wormhole (Morris & Thorne 1988,
  Sec. III): a static observer anywhere needs no proper acceleration to remain at fixed r.
- `"inverse"`: `Phi(r) = -r0/r` — finite everywhere, `-> 0` as `r -> infinity`, a standard
  illustrative non-zero-redshift example.

**Radius grid**: `r` sampled at `r0` times the multiples
`{1.0, 1.01, 1.1, 1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, 1.9, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 50.0}` (`1.0` = the throat itself;
the cluster between 1.5 and 2.0 was added specifically to resolve any sign changes in that
region at adequate resolution, rather than only sampling coarsely on either side of it).

The full grid (2 throat radii × 4 shape exponents × 2 redshift presets × 12 radius multiples =
192 cells) is generated by `scripts/generate_registry.py` and frozen in
`reports/v0.1-wormhole-registry.json`.

## Derived quantities computed at every grid point

1. **Stress-energy** `(rho, p_r, p_t)`, from the orthonormal-frame Einstein tensor of the
   metric above (`src/wormhole_lab/curvature.py`) — the standard result for a general static,
   spherically symmetric metric (e.g. Misner, Thorne & Wheeler, *Gravitation*, Sec. 23.5),
   specialized to this wormhole ansatz as in Morris & Thorne (1988), eqs. 10–12.
2. **Energy conditions**: NEC (`rho+p_r >= 0` and `rho+p_t >= 0`), WEC (NEC and `rho >= 0`),
   SEC (NEC and `rho+p_r+2p_t >= 0`) — standard definitions (e.g. Visser, *Lorentzian
   Wormholes*, Ch. 12).
3. **Embedding geometry**: proper radial distance `l(r)` and embedding height `z(r)`
   (Morris & Thorne 1988, eqs. 3–4a), by numerical quadrature.
4. **Static-observer proper acceleration**, `a(r) = Phi'(r) sqrt(1 - b(r)/r)` — the
   acceleration a *non-moving* observer at r must maintain against gravity. This is
   *explicitly not* a full traveler tidal-force analysis (see Limitations, and
   `src/wormhole_lab/tidal.py`'s module docstring).

## Hypotheses and pass/fail criteria (stated before the registry was interpreted)

- **H1 (flare-out forces NEC violation at the throat):** for every tested `(r0, n, redshift)`
  combination with `n > 0` (a genuine, non-degenerate throat), `rho + p_r < 0` exactly at
  `r = r0`. *Falsified if* any tested combination has `rho + p_r >= 0` at the throat.
- **H2 (the zero-tidal-force preset requires exotic matter everywhere, not just at the
  throat):** for the `"zero"` redshift preset, `rho + p_r < 0` at *every* tested radius, not
  only near the throat. *Falsified if* any tested radius under this preset shows
  `rho + p_r >= 0`.
- **H3 (a non-zero redshift function can, in principle, localize the violation):** for the
  `"inverse"` redshift preset, there exists at least one tested radius where the full NEC
  (`rho+p_r >= 0` and `rho+p_t >= 0`) is satisfied. *Falsified if* no tested radius under this
  preset satisfies the full NEC.
- **H4 (asymptotic flatness):** `rho, p_r, p_t -> 0` as `r -> infinity`, for every tested shape
  function (a basic sanity requirement — the spacetime must approach flat space far from the
  throat). *Falsified if* any tested configuration fails to show `|rho|`, `|p_r|`, `|p_t|`
  monotonically decreasing toward the largest sampled radius.

## Independent correctness checks (not about physical reality — about the implementation)

Because there is no external dataset to validate this implementation against, two independent
mathematical checks are used instead, both preregistered here and enforced in
`tests/test_curvature.py`:

1. **Closed-form reduction**: setting `Phi' = Phi'' = 0` in the general formulas must exactly
   reproduce the simpler, widely reproduced "zero-tidal-force" closed forms
   `rho = b'/(8*pi*r^2)`, `p_r = -b/(8*pi*r^3)`, `p_t = (b - r*b')/(16*pi*r^3)`.
2. **Conservation identity**: any `(rho, p_r, p_t, Phi)` sourced by this metric via Einstein's
   equations must satisfy `dp_r/dr = -(rho+p_r) Phi' + (2/r)(p_t-p_r)` as a consequence of the
   Bianchi identity, independent of the specific matter model. This is checked numerically
   (finite-difference `dp_r/dr` against the right-hand side) at multiple points for every
   tested shape/redshift combination.
3. **Embedding quadrature vs. closed form**: for the `n=1` shape function, both `l(r)` and
   `z(r)` have known closed forms (`sqrt(r^2-r0^2)` and `r0*arccosh(r/r0)` respectively); the
   numerical quadrature is checked against these.

An implementation that failed any of these three checks would not be trusted regardless of
what the registry showed.

## Scope boundaries, declared before results

- Classical General Relativity only. No quantum-gravitational, semiclassical backreaction, or
  modified-gravity treatment of whether the required stress-energy could actually be sourced.
- Static and spherically symmetric solutions only. No rotating (Teo-type), dynamical, or
  non-spherical wormhole geometries.
- No traveler-frame tidal-force analysis. Only the static-observer proper acceleration is
  computed; a moving traveler's tidal forces require the Riemann tensor boosted into their
  rest frame (Morris & Thorne 1988, Sec. V), which this v0.1 does not implement.
- A disclosed, finite grid of shape/redshift functions and radii — not an exhaustive search
  over the (infinite-dimensional) space of valid `(b, Phi)` pairs.
- No claim, anywhere in this repository, that traversable wormholes exist, have been observed,
  or can be engineered.
